02Research Questions

Three questions form my initial agenda. Tap a card to turn it over, then use + to see what I want to solve.

Question 1

When do linguistic cues help distinguish false news from satire and legitimate reporting, and when do they become misleading?

The goalFind out whether a model learns useful distinctions or leans on a publisher, topic, or writing style. I’d compare interpretable linguistic features with text-representation models, test on publishers and topics held out of training, and look closely at satire labeled as false news and legitimate reporting flagged for emotional language. The aim: clarify when linguistic evidence supports a label, and when a system should express uncertainty instead.

Question 2

How can suspicious review activity be identified when there are too few verified examples of manipulation to train a dependable classifier?

The goalStart in a bounded setting, such as reviews of one category of health-related apps. Examine repeated language, timing, rating patterns, and reviewer–app relationships where data are legitimately available, and compare text-only methods with behavioral or network signals. Similarity alone is not deception, so evaluation needs documented annotation, independent judgments, and explicit handling of uncertain cases. The aim: flag activity that warrants investigation without claiming it proves fraud.

Question 3

How should a platform explain a credibility warning so that people understand the evidence without treating it as a final verdict?

The goalWith appropriate training and ethics approval, run a controlled study comparing a simple warning, an evidence-based explanation, and no warning, using content whose status is independently established. That lets me evaluate participants’ actual judgments, including mistaken rejection of legitimate content and acceptance of misleading content. The aim: learn whether explanations improve appropriate reliance on a system, including noticing its mistakes.

A note on scopeThese questions are an initial agenda, not a claim that I’ve found unexplored gaps. I’d review the literature in depth first and refine a feasible starting problem with an advisor, narrowing or reconsidering a question as the evidence calls for.

03How the Questions Fit Together

  1. STEP 01 · Q1 + Q2

    What evidence can a system offer?

    Study the linguistic and review-activity signals that are genuinely informative, and where they mislead.

  2. STEP 02 · Q3

    How is that evidence understood?

    Test how people interpret explanations and warnings, measuring judgments rather than only stated helpfulness.

  3. THROUGHOUT

    Honest about uncertainty

    Prefer flagging for further investigation over confident verdicts, and document every annotation and experiment.
